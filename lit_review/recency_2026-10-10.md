# Recency sweep: 2026-09-15 to 2026-10-10

Companion to `00_preemption_sweep_2026-10-10.md`. Window: arXiv submissions dated 2026-09-15 through 2026-10-10 (today).

Method. (a) arXiv API `search_query` with `submittedDate:[202609150000 TO 202610102359]`, twelve keyword combinations (multilingual/cross-lingual x agent/deep research/browsecomp/web search; Indic language names x retrieval/RAG/search; "query language"/"query translation"; "language preference"/"language bias"/"knowledge disparit*"; "oracle retrieval"/"evidence gap"/"retrieval gap"; agentic search / search agent / deep research alone), restricted to cs.CL/cs.IR/cs.AI where the unrestricted query was noisy (`abs:Indic` stems to "indicate", `abs:search` pulls physics). ~330 unique titles scanned. (b) WebFetch of `arxiv.org/list/cs.IR/recent` and `cs.CL/recent` (both cover only 5-9 Oct; cs.CL truncated at ~25% of the page, so the API is the primary instrument). (c) WebSearch (extended) on the seven prescribed phrasings plus five variants. (d) MAST site, HF dataset cards. Abstracts of every candidate below were read on arxiv.org; nothing is summarised from search snippets alone.

Novelty pins being tested: (i) the agent's search-query language as a measured, then forced, mediator; (ii) a coverage x conditional-accuracy decomposition on native Indic evidence with parallel questions and a controlled evidence pool (English-only / Indic-only / both).

Threat scale: none = different problem or already accounted for in the seed sweep; partial = overlaps one pin or supplies vocabulary a reviewer will expect us to position against; severe = runs our factorial or an Indic agentic equivalent.

## A. Relevant hits (ranked by proximity)

### 1. arXiv:2610.00606 -- Where's Waldo? Query-language Preference under Cross-lingual Knowledge Disparities
Ki, Zhang, Cucerzan, White, Gao. Submitted 2026-09-30. cs.CL. 43 pp.
Waldo is a ~12K-pair Wikipedia QA benchmark in fr, zh, ja, ko and **bn**, built from post-2020 revisions so that each fact exists in the source-language article but is absent from English (a "knowledge gap", found via Wikidata QID matching) or is deliberately contradicted in an LLM-edited English copy (a "knowledge conflict"). Eight models receive a fixed paired context (source-language doc + English doc) and the query language is varied between the source language and English; "query-language preference" is the agreement/probability gap between the two query languages. Open-weight models show 12-18 pt preference gaps under knowledge gaps; under conflicts all models follow the query-language document (mean gap 55.7 pts); Bengali has the largest probability gaps (LLaMA-3.1 bn: 91.4% source-language vs 78.5% English agreement under gaps; 80.6% vs 14.0% under conflicts). Mitigations: attention-head ablation and QLoRA (up to -61.5% gap). Retrieval is single-pass fixed context; one appendix adds OpenAI web-search documents as a robustness check. No iterative agent, no logging or forcing of the language the model *searches* in, no coverage term, Bengali is the only Indic language.
**Threat to our novelty: partial.** It occupies the vocabulary "query-language preference" and "knowledge gap" and shows an evidence-only-in-one-language construction with Bengali, so it must be cited and positioned against. It does not touch pin (i) (there is no agent-issued query) and does not do pin (ii) (no coverage x conditional-accuracy decomposition, evidence is handed to the model). Same first author as Linguistic Nepotism (arXiv:2509.13930); the group is clearly moving along this line, which argues for speed.

### 2. arXiv:2610.03136 -- Investigating the Role of Reasoning-Language Alignment in Monolingual Retrieval-Augmented Generation
Hauck, Sanz-Guerrero, von der Wense. Submitted 2026-10-02. cs.CL.
A fully German agentic RAG testbed (The Dark Eye RPG lore, too niche for parametric recall) in which the *reasoning* language of the agent is forced (German / French / unconstrained English). Aligning reasoning language with query+evidence language helps (forced German beats forced French despite the model being stronger in French), but forced German only matches unconstrained English reasoning. Monolingual by design; de/fr/en only; no Indic; the manipulated variable is reasoning language, not search-query language; no evidence-pool manipulation.
**Threat: partial (weak).** It is the closest "force the agent's internal language and measure" design in the window, and reviewers may ask why we force the query language rather than the reasoning language. Positioning: our mediator is the externally observable search string, and our evidence pool varies.

### 3. MAST @ FIRE 2026 (Multilingual Agentic Search Track), Indic track -- status check
mast-benchmark.github.io; HF `mast-benchmark/indic-queries-2026` (9 Indic langs x 50 encrypted queries, English BrowseComp-Plus corpus, English answers). Timeline as of today: runs closed 2026-09-25; results and public leaderboard 2026-09-30; **working notes camera-ready 2026-10-15; overview paper due 2026-10-30**. The site leaderboard currently shows only organiser baselines (gpt-oss-20b 39.47% EM, Tongyi-DR-30B + Qwen3-8B 39.07%, Tongyi-DR-30B + mLateOn 33.73%, DR-Tulu-8B 13.33%, Track 1); `/leaderboard` returns 404; no participant notes or overview on arXiv yet (API query `FIRE AND (multilingual OR Indic)` returns nothing in window). One participant-side artefact exists: HF `nirantk/mast-fire-2026-gemini-translations` (MIT; Gemini translations of the MAST queries across ~21 languages, tagged mast / browsecomp-plus / fire-2026), i.e. at least one team used LLM query translation to English as a strategy despite the organisers discouraging translation as the *only* strategy.
**Threat: partial (timing).** Unchanged from the seed sweep: the corpus is English-only so no evidence gap can be measured, but participant notes (from 2026-10-15) will contain query-translation vs native-query comparisons on nine Indic languages, which is half of pin (i) in shared-task form.

### 5. arXiv:2610.04899 -- Rewrite What Matters: Adaptive Multilingual Query Rewriting for Reasoning via Agentic Reinforcement Learning (mRewriter-R1)
Qi, Chen, Liang, Meng, Lu, Shi, Xu, Meng, Huang. Submitted 2026-10-04. cs.CL.
RL-trained multi-turn query rewriter that chooses among rewriting operators (translation is one) so that semantically equivalent queries in different languages lead to the same reasoning trajectory. Reasoning benchmarks, not retrieval or web search; languages not given in the abstract; no Indic named. Same first author as LcRL / CroSearch-R1 (seed sweep sec. 2).
**Threat: none** for the decomposition; **partial** as an adjacent method: "should the agent translate its query" is exactly the policy knob they learn, albeit for reasoning. Cite alongside CroSearch-R1.

### 6. arXiv:2609.24407 -- Auditing Source Exposure in Baidu and Google AI Search
Li, Guan, Cai, Liu, Pierri. Submitted 2026-09-21. cs.IR.
Cross-lingual audit of AI overviews using MS MARCO English queries and their Chinese translations on Baidu and Google; overview availability and visible source exposure differ substantially by platform-language setting. Query language is a factor, but the object is a commercial AI-overview product, en/zh only, no agent, no accuracy.
**Threat: none.** Adjacent to Zatuchin (arXiv:2608.30052) and Sources of Truth (arXiv:2609.00319) in the seed sweep.

### 7. arXiv:2610.02926 -- Output Language Confusion under Multilingual Prompt Contamination
Marwah, Garimella, Bansal, Jain, Sheth. Submitted 2026-10-02. cs.CL.
Factual questions preceded by foreign-language distractor sentences (motivated by RAG pipelines returning mixed-language passages); Llama-3.1-8B switches to Devanagari output 58% of the time under Hindi distractors; others abstain. Output-language confusion, not query language; closed-book.
**Threat: none.** Usable as a Hindi-specific language-confusion citation next to Marchisio et al.

### 8. arXiv:2609.18445 -- M-SQE: Multilingual Skill Quality Estimation for Enhancing Language Equality in Agentic Skill Use
Liu et al. (12 authors). Submitted 2026-09-16. cs.CL.
Audit finds skill libraries have no in-language content for Hindi or Swahili, so retrieval returns skills in a language other than the query's; proposes post-retrieval quality estimation; +12.9 pp on Hindi. A "corpus has nothing in your language" finding for agent skills rather than for factual evidence.
**Threat: none**, but it is a clean recent example of an evidence-coverage gap for Hindi in an agentic setting; worth one sentence in related work.

## C. Overall judgement
No severe threat appeared between 2026-09-15 and 2026-10-10. One new partial threat: **Waldo (arXiv:2610.00606)** owns the phrase "query-language preference" and demonstrates a knowledge-gap-by-construction Wikipedia design that includes Bengali, but at the level of fixed context handed to the model, not an agent that chooses what to search and in which language, and with no coverage decomposition. MAST remains a timing risk (notes 2026-10-15, overview 2026-10-30). Verdict unchanged: **ALIVE-BUT-CROWDED**; the two pins hold. Two writing consequences: (1) do not describe our mediator as "query-language preference" (Waldo) or our gap as a "retrieval gap" (Liu et al. 2608.01913) or "exposure" (2609.38670); use "query-side gap" / "evidence-side gap" as the seed sweep suggests; (2) cite Waldo as the single-shot, fixed-context precursor whose Bengali result (largest preference gap) motivates testing the same phenomenon when the agent, not the experimenter, chooses the evidence language.
