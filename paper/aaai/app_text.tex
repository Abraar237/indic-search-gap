\section{Coverage measurement and audit}
\label{app:coverage}
For each of the 539 XorQA-In test questions per language we resolved the English source article to its Hindi or Telugu counterpart through Wikidata sitelinks (916 of 918 distinct titles resolved; two had no Wikidata item) and looked the counterpart up in the 20261001 snapshot. An article exists for 364 Hindi and 283 Telugu items. Coverage was then decided in three steps. (1) \emph{String match}: the Indic gold answer, or the English gold answer, occurs in the article after NFC normalisation and punctuation stripping; 119 Hindi and 94 Telugu items (the 237 Telugu items whose released translated answer is the function word ``\emph{ani}'' were matched on the English answer only). (2) \emph{Two independent LLM reads}: gemini-2.5-flash-lite and then gemini-3.6-flash were each given the question in both languages, the gold answers and the full article (up to 14,000 characters) and asked to quote verbatim the sentence that states the answer or reply NONE; a read counts only if the quoted sentence is found in the article. The first read accepted 205 Hindi and 179 Telugu items; the second accepted 158 and 129; both agree on 157 and 127, which are the verified-coverage counts (29.1\% and 23.6\%). (3) \emph{Manual audit}: before the second read, we drew 25 items per language stratified over first-read outcomes and labelled them by reading the quoted sentence against the question. Of the 30 first-read acceptances, 21 were correct, 7 were uncertain (the quote did not visibly contain the answer) and 2 were wrong (the Hindi article gives 13 South American countries where the gold says twelve; the Telugu Gandhi article never names Subhas Chandra Bose). The second read rejected 1 of the 2 wrong, 4 of the 7 uncertain and 8 of the 21 correct acceptances, so the final coverage figure is conservative: it under-counts rather than over-counts. The 20 first-read rejections in the audit were not labelled by hand; the second read accepted 3 of them with a verbatim quote, but an item needs both reads to count, so they stay outside the controlled grid and the coverage figure. The audit file with every label is shipped as \texttt{results/coverage\_audit.json}.
\section{Judge audit}
\label{app:audit}
Of the judged agent answers, the string rule decided 41\% (exact or contained match to a gold alias in either script after digit normalisation); the rest went to the LLM judge (gemini-2.5-flash, not in the grid), which returned CORRECT, WRONG or ABSTAIN. We drew 40 judged answers (20 per language: 10 string-correct, 5 LLM-correct, 5 LLM-wrong or abstain) and labelled them by hand against the question and gold aliases. All 40 verdicts agreed with the manual label. Cases worth noting: a Korean-script answer accepted for the romanised gold ``mumu''; ``labour movement'' accepted for ``trade union movement''; an answer naming one of three states in the gold marked WRONG, which we consider defensible; and seven abstentions (``the search results do not contain\ldots'') marked ABSTAIN and scored as wrong. The sample is shipped as \texttt{results/audit\_sample.json}.
