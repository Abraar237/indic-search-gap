Search in Whose Language?

Separating the query-side gap from the evidence-side gap for search agents in Indic languages · FROZEN 2026-10-10

Question

When a user asks an LLM search agent a question in Hindi or Telugu, how much of the accuracy loss relative to the same question in English comes from the agent's own query behaviour (query-side gap), how much from the evidence not existing in that language (evidence-side gap), and how much from reading the evidence (integration gap)? In what language do agents search when left free, and does forcing the query language recover the loss?

AccL = CovL · Acc(L | evidence in L) + (1 − CovL) · Acc(L | evidence only in English)

CovL is measured on the live Indic Wikipedia snapshot; the conditional accuracies come from the controlled grid below.

Pre-registered directions (recorded 2026-10-10 16:10, before any grid call)

IdDirection
D1Under a free policy with an Indic question, agents issue English queries in more than half of search calls; more for Telugu than Hindi.
D2The query-side gap exists; the dual policy recovers at least half; English-only recovers less than dual.
D3Forcing English queries helps when evidence is English-only and hurts when it is Indic-only (policy × evidence interaction).
D4Covte < 0.5 and Covhi > Covte; the coverage term exceeds the agent term for Telugu but not Hindi.
D5The Indic deficit in the symmetric monolingual check is larger under BM25 than under bge-m3 and shrinks under oracle passages.
D6Under oracle evidence the Indic question is within 5 points of the English question for closed models.

Chunks of ~900 characters, title-prefixed. Retriever: BM25 (bm25s, Unicode tokeniser, no stemming); second retriever bge-m3 on the headline cells. One tool: search(query) → top-5 passages.

Agent and factors

ReAct loop over OpenRouter tool calling; at most 3 searches, then a short answer in the question's language; temperature 0; reasoning effort minimal. The policy sentence is the only prompt difference between cells.

Scoring and statistics

Alias-aware string rule in both scripts (digit normalisation for Devanagari and Telugu numerals), then an LLM judge (gemini-2.5-flash, not in the grid) only where the rule says wrong; 40 judged items hand-audited. Unit = item; paired contrasts; sign-flip permutation (20k); item bootstrap CIs (10k); interactions by label permutation (5k); Benjamini-Hochberg over the full family; noise floor beside every headline.
